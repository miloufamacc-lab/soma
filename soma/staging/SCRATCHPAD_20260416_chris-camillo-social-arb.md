## RED TEAM (Phase 2.5 — top 8+ impact claims challenged)

### Counter to C1 (Track Record, impact 10)
- **Counter:** Self-commissioned audits are not SEC-reviewed performance reports. Covester had 40,000 accounts during his #1 period — at that sample size, the statistically expected top-1% annualized return is itself extreme. "75% over 18 years" is ~5 standard deviations above the index average — the prior for a true alpha generator at this level is roughly 1 in 10,000 active traders.
- **Evidence:** Academic literature (Barber & Odean 2000; Fama-French factor studies) consistently shows <1% of active retail traders outperform the market on a risk-adjusted basis over 10+ year windows.
- **Steelman strength: MODERATE.** The trades he publicly described (PLTR, SPHR, ELF, COVID WFH basket) are real and directionally confirmed. But total-portfolio 75% requires more than picking winners — it requires timing + sizing executing correctly at every rebalance, which is harder to verify.

### Counter to C2 (Info asymmetry → parity, impact 9)
- **Counter:** Markets can stay inefficient far longer than a single trade window. Underreaction can persist through multiple earnings cycles; overreaction can snap at exit. "Parity" is a qualitative call, not a triggerable event.
- **Evidence:** Post-Earnings Announcement Drift literature shows price adjustment to new info often takes 60+ days.
- **Steelman strength: WEAK.** Camillo's method works even with slow parity — he's trading directional exposure, not closing-price precision. This counter is weak.

### Counter to C3 ($30M COVID trade, impact 9)
- **Counter:** COVID was a once-in-50-years vol event. A 370% year does not replicate. Attribution: 60-70% of return came from timing of the reversal (buying 14-15 names at the March 2020 bottom), not alt-data. Most of the edge was courage + liquidity, not information.
- **Evidence:** Anyone long Peloton/Shopify/Amazon from late March 2020 to year-end made multi-hundred-percent returns; this was broadly available, not alpha.
- **Steelman strength: STRONG.** The COVID year is probably the weakest support for the "repeatable methodology" argument — the edge was behavioral (aggressively deploying capital at a bottom), not informational.

### Counter to C5 (Palantir, impact 9)
- **Counter:** PLTR's run from $30 to $180 happened in a regime where every AI-adjacent name got rerated. Identifying "AI wave winner" in mid-2024 was not a deep insight; it was consensus. Camillo's timing-on-thesis was good but the information asymmetry was weaker than claimed.
- **Evidence:** CROWD, NVDA, ANET, VST all moved 3-5x in the same window on similar narrative.
- **Steelman strength: MODERATE.** PLTR specifically did move faster than most AI-infrastructure peers in 2H2024-1H2025, and the "commercial AIP adoption as discovery catalyst" was genuinely not widely understood at $30. Informational edge real but smaller than narrative implies.

### Counter to C6 (Bloom Energy, impact 8)
- **Counter:** BE has already run 5-6x on the AI-data-center narrative. Fuel cell technology has had false dawns since 2001 (BE IPO 2018 at $15, down to $1.89 in 2020, now ~$60 — classic boom-bust). Hyperscaler concentration (one Oracle deal) is risk, not validation. Sell-side consensus is ~30% revenue growth, not 100%+ earnings doubling.
- **Evidence:** BE FY24 EPS ~$0.15 consensus. Doubling YoY for 4 years would require FY28 EPS of $2.40 — implies revenue of ~$5B+ vs. current $1.6B run-rate. That's achievable only if hyperscaler win rate steps up materially.
- **Steelman strength: STRONG.** Base rate on energy-technology forward bets is poor; BE specifically has a 20-year track record of disappointing. High skepticism warranted.

### Counter to C8 (QSR loss, impact 8)
- **Counter:** Camillo frames this as a diligence failure. A more honest reading: the methodology structurally under-samples negative signals because the TikTok/YouTube alpha layer doesn't exist for low-conversation brands like Tim Hortons. The methodology has systematic blind spots for Canadian/regional/non-consumer-tech brands — and he levered into one of them anyway.
- **Evidence:** QSR's franchisee revolt WAS publicly reported in trade press weeks before earnings. Research failure, not alt-data failure.
- **Steelman strength: MODERATE.** Good self-critique accepted; his own frame (be more comprehensive) is valid. But the deeper point — that his method has a demographic blind spot HE uses as his edge elsewhere — should be mentioned more often.

### Counter to C10 (5-10% position sizing, impact 8)
- **Counter:** This understates tail risk. QSR proves the 5-10% "option premium at risk" framing is wrong for high-conviction levered options — when the underlying drops hard AND implied vol collapses, premium decay plus delta loss can wipe out the full position AND pull more from margin. 5-10% premium ≠ 5-10% portfolio loss in adversarial scenarios.
- **Evidence:** Multi-leg levered options positions with 10% premium can routinely lose 15-25% of NAV in gap-down scenarios because of margin-called directional legs.
- **Steelman strength: STRONG.** This is a structural understatement that matters for retail listeners who take the sizing advice literally.

### Counter to C19 (Valuation irrelevance, impact 8, implicit)
- **Counter:** PLTR's run happened during a discount-rate tailwind (falling 10yr yields narrative). In a rate-regime reversal, even correct information trades get crushed by multiple compression. Ignoring valuation is regime-dependent.
- **Evidence:** 2022 SaaS de-rating: companies that beat earnings still lost 30-60% on multiple compression.
- **Steelman strength: STRONG.** His method probably worked for 2007-2025 in part because of a secular decline in discount rates. It is not tested in a sustained high-real-rate regime.
